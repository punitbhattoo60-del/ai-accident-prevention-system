About the Project

An extra set of eyes, exactly where drivers are blind.

Most serious crashes at intersections don’t happen because drivers are careless — they happen because the hazard was physically hidden: behind a parked truck, a building corner, or another vehicle. This system moves the “eyes” to the roadside, where the full scene is visible.

The concept

A fixed roadside camera continuously observes a blind intersection. A YOLO-based detection model identifies vehicles and pedestrians in every frame, tracks their movement, and estimates whether their paths will collide. When risk crosses a threshold, a warning is sent to the approaching vehicle — giving its driver time to slow down before the hazard appears.

The current prototype demonstrates this full loop in simulation: detection, risk calculation, inter-vehicle hazard communication, and an early driver warning.

Project objectives

  • Detect pedestrians and vehicles hidden from a driver’s view at blind spots and intersections.
  • Calculate collision risk from object position, direction and closing speed.
  • Communicate hazard information from one vehicle / roadside unit to another vehicle.
  • Deliver an early, clear warning so the driver can brake or slow down in time.
  • Demonstrate the complete prevention loop in a working prototype and simulation.
Problem Statement

Drivers can only react to what they can see.

By the time a hidden hazard becomes visible, there is often less than a second left to react. Four failure modes cause most of these crashes:

Blind spots

Buildings, parked vehicles and road geometry create corners where an entire lane of traffic is invisible until the last moment.

Hidden pedestrians

A pedestrian stepping out from behind a bus or truck is visible to a roadside camera long before the driver can see them.

Sudden vehicle obstacles

A vehicle emerging fast from a side lane or driveway gives the driver on the main road almost no braking distance.

Poor visibility & slow reaction

Fog, rain, darkness and glare shrink visibility further — and human reaction time adds roughly a second before braking even begins.

How It Works

From camera frame to driver warning, in six steps.

STEP 1

Camera detects

A roadside camera observes the blind intersection and captures pedestrians and vehicles the driver cannot see.

STEP 2

AI identifies objects

YOLO, running on OpenCV frames, classifies and tracks each object — vehicle, pedestrian — with bounding boxes and confidence scores.

STEP 3

Risk is calculated

The system estimates trajectories and closing speeds. If two paths intersect within a critical time window, risk escalates: Low → Medium → High.

STEP 4

Hazard is communicated

The hazard information — what, where, and how urgent — is transmitted to the other approaching vehicle (Vehicle B).

STEP 5

Driver is warned early

Vehicle B’s driver receives a visual and audible early warning while the hazard is still hidden — gaining seconds to slow down.

STEP 6

Collision is prevented

The prototype demonstrates the outcome: the warned vehicle slows in time, and the collision that would have happened, doesn’t.

Benefits & Applications

Where this system makes roads safer.

⬢

Smart cities

Roadside units at dangerous junctions become part of city safety infrastructure, feeding traffic and hazard data to the network.

↗

Highway safety

Early warning of sudden obstacles, stopped vehicles and merge conflicts at highway speeds, where reaction time matters most.

◎

Pedestrian protection

The people most at risk — pedestrians hidden from drivers — are exactly the ones a roadside camera can see first.

⬣

Autonomous vehicles

Infrastructure-side perception complements on-board sensors, giving self-driving systems information beyond their own line of sight.

✛

Blind intersection safety

The core use case: T-junctions, building corners and parked-vehicle obstructions where neither driver can see the other.

◉

Fleet & public transport

Buses and trucks have the largest blind spots of all — roadside warnings protect both their drivers and everyone around them.

Future Scope

The roadmap from prototype to road.

Everything below is planned work — it is not part of the current prototype and is not claimed as implemented.

01

Real-time hardware integration Planned

Replace simulated feeds with a live roadside camera stream processed in real time.

02

ESP32 communication Planned

ESP32 units in vehicles receive hazard messages wirelessly and drive the in-vehicle warning display and buzzer.

03

Automatic emergency braking Future

Escalate from warning the driver to actuating braking when the driver does not respond in time.

04

Advanced collision prediction Future

Trajectory forecasting over longer horizons, with weather, speed and road-condition awareness.

05

Multi-vehicle communication (V2X) Future

One hazard broadcast to every approaching vehicle — a cooperative safety network, not just a single warning.
